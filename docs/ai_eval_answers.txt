  Harvest check limit 1: The sample is small: 25 seasons have a harvest, and 3 of them finished below the payment line.
  Harvest check limit 2: A typhoon can cut a harvest in a year that was not dry.
  Harvest check limit 3: Pests and disease can cut a harvest with no change in the season's rainfall.
  Harvest check limit 4: Seeds and varieties changed over these years, and newer varieties yield more.
  Harvest check limit 5: Most Nueva Ecija rice is irrigated, so a dry spell at one map point may not change the provincial harvest.
  Harvest check limit 6: Prices and how much land is planted change the harvest as well as the weather.
  Harvest check limit 7: The harvest figure is the July to December crop. The rainfall season is June to October, so the two calendars do not match exactly.
  Harvest check limit 8: This comparison does not change the payment line, the model, or the trigger.
  Limit: estimate: Estimates are rough before week 12.
  Limit: farm: Weather at this map point is not the same as weather on one farm.
  Limit: prototype: This is a prototype and a simulation. It is not a regulated lending product.
  Scope: RainCheck covers ONE crop in ONE season: wet-season rice (June to October). It does not cover multi-year crops such as coconut or fruit trees, the dry-season rice crop, or losses from typhoons, floods, pests or disease. We chose a single-season crop on purpose: one planting-to-harvest cycle makes a season-total rainfall rule easier to define and to check.
